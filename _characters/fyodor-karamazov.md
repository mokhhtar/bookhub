Fyodor Pavlovich Karamazov is a central figure in Fyodor Dostoevsky’s 1879–1880 novel, The Brothers Karamazov. As the father of Alexei, Ivan, and Dmitri, and the rumored father of his servant Pavel Fyodorovich Smerdyakov, he is depicted as a self-indulgent and shameless libertine. He shows no concern for the responsibilities of fatherhood or the welfare of his children, serving as a catalyst for deep moral questions regarding filial obligation.

The character’s complex relationships with his sons drive much of the novel’s plot, particularly his intense conflict with his eldest son, Dmitri, and his significant, evolving connection with Ivan. Through these interactions, Dostoevsky explores the complexities and moral ambiguities of the father-son dynamic, using the Karamazov family to reflect on the broader reality of Russian social disintegration.
