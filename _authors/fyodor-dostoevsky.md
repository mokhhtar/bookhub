Fyodor Mikhailovich Dostoevsky was a Russian novelist, philosopher, short story writer, essayist, and journalist. Widely regarded as one of the greatest writers in both Russian and world literature, his influential works explore the human condition within the troubled political, social, and spiritual atmospheres of 19th-century Russia. His writing frequently engages with a variety of philosophical and religious themes.

Dostoevsky’s most acclaimed novels include Crime and Punishment, The Idiot, Demons, The Adolescent, and The Brothers Karamazov. Additionally, his 1864 novella Notes from Underground is considered one of the first works of existentialist literature.
