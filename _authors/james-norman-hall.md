James Norman Hall was an American writer best known for The Bounty Trilogy, a series of historical novels co-authored with Charles Bernard Nordhoff that includes Mutiny on the Bounty, Men Against the Sea, and Pitcairn's Island. During World War I, Hall uniquely served in the armed forces of three Allied nations, acting as an infantryman for Great Britain and an aviator for both France and the United States. His distinguished wartime service earned him the Croix de Guerre, the Médaille Militaire, the Légion d'Honneur, and the Distinguished Service Cross.

After the war, Hall settled in Tahiti, where he and Nordhoff collaborated on a number of successful adventure novels, many of which were adapted into films. He was also the father of Conrad L. Hall, a three-time Academy Award-winning cinematographer.
