José Saramago was a Portuguese writer whose works often present subversive perspectives on historic events, frequently utilizing allegory to emphasize the theopoetic human factor. In 1998, he was awarded the Nobel Prize in Literature for his parables, which are sustained by imagination, compassion, and irony, and which enable readers to apprehend an elusory reality.

The author has received significant critical acclaim, with Harold Bloom describing him in 2003 as the most gifted novelist alive and later naming him a permanent part of the Western canon. Additionally, James Wood has praised the distinctive tone of Saramago’s fiction, noting that he narrates his novels with a voice that is simultaneously wise and ignorant.
